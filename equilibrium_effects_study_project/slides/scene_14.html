CORE CONCEPTUAL FOCUS

Desires Versus Transactions: Why 'Supply Equals Demand' is a Theory

Quantity supplied and demanded reflect desires at price p, not transacted quantities. If defined as transactions, supply trivially equals demand; as behavioral desires, it is a testable theory.

Theoretical Distinction
Quantity supplied is what firms want to sell; quantity demanded is what consumers want to buy
Accounting Identity Fallacy
Confusing actual transacted sales (which always match) with behavioral market curves
Predictive Power
The supply-and-demand model succeeds because it accurately predicts the shortages caused by price controls
METHODOLOGICAL EPISTEMOLOGY Wants vs Transactions
Economic Definition

Behavioral Desires

Quantity Supplied: What firms want to sell at price p, holding other factors constant.

Quantity Demanded: What consumers want to buy at price p, holding other factors constant.

"Supply Equals Demand" is a TESTABLE THEORY
Journalistic Fallacy

Accounting Identities

If defined as actual quantities transacted, supply always equals demand by definition, because every sale has a buyer and a seller.

Confusing wants with transactions makes people wrongly believe shortages cannot exist.
Trivial Accounting Identity
The Power of the Model: Because it models wants, microeconomics accurately predicts excess demand under price caps.

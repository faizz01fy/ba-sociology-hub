CORE CONCEPTUAL FOCUS

Direct Price Controls: Price Ceilings and Price Floors

Rather than shifting curves, governments directly mandate legal price boundaries. Price ceilings set statutory maximums, while price floors set statutory minimums.

Direct Price Control
Statutory regulation prohibiting transactions outside designated price boundaries
Price Ceiling (p_bar)
Legal maximum price; binding only when set strictly below equilibrium price (p_bar < p*)
Price Floor (p_bar)
Legal minimum price; binding only when set strictly above equilibrium price (p_bar > p*)

Binding Condition: p̄ < p*
Non-Binding Condition: p̄ ≥ p*
Market Result: Shortage (Qd > Qs)
Examples: Rent control, gasoline price caps
Price Floor

Legal Minimum (p ≥ p̄)

Statutory lower boundary on retail transactions.

Binding Condition: p̄ > p*
Non-Binding Condition: p̄ ≤ p*
Market Result: Surplus (Qs > Qd)
Examples: Minimum wage, agricultural price supports
Analytical Rule: A price control is only binding when it blocks the market from reaching natural equilibrium.

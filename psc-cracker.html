PSC Cracker

A separate study space for planning Kerala PSC revision, finding official exam information, and reviewing practice answers carefully.

Independent study guide. Academic Hub is not the Kerala Public Service Commission (KPSC/PSC). This page does not host or claim to provide an official question bank.

Start with the exact post and notification

PSC exams differ by post and notification. Before you practise, write down the post name, notification number, syllabus version, and exam pattern shown in the current official notice. A paper for a different post may be useful for general practice, but it is not a substitute for the syllabus that applies to your application.

  1. Open the Kerala PSC official website and use its current menus or site search to find the relevant notification, syllabus, previous question paper, or answer key.
  2. Match every paper to its post, year, exam date, and question-paper code before starting.
  3. Attempt the paper without notes. Follow the time limit and scoring rules stated in the current notification.
  4. Check answers against the latest official key available for that paper. If a key has been revised, use the latest published version and keep its status with your notes.
  5. Record why each wrong answer happened, then revisit that topic from the syllabus or a reliable source.

Read answer keys by status

Key labelHow to use it
ProvisionalUse it for an initial check only. It may change after the objection process.
FinalUse the final key published for the matching paper code as your main reference.
Revised or rectifiedCheck whether a later version replaces an earlier key, and note the publication date/version in your review log.

Do not compare your answers with a key from a different paper code or rely on an unattributed answer list when an official key is available.

Keep a useful error log

A score shows where you are today; a short error log shows what to study next. For every missed or guessed question, record the topic, the reason you chose your answer, the corrected idea, and the official source or syllabus point you used to check it. Review the same errors again after a few days.

  • Knowledge gap: I did not know the concept or fact.
  • Reading mistake: I missed a word such as “not,” “only,” or “except.”
  • Reasoning mistake: I knew the topic but did not compare the options carefully.
  • Unverified guess: I selected an answer without being able to explain why it was correct.
